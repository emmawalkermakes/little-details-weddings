Wedding stationery has a habit of looking simple until you count the steps: wording approval, guest address list, printing, delivery, assembly, and postage that has to land before anyone books flights. Australian couples also plan around summer heat, humidity and guests travelling interstate or from overseas.

This is an honest planning guide for personalised stationery — save-the-dates through thank-yous — working **backwards from your wedding date**. It is not a price list. Lead times vary by supplier, season and how custom the job is, so treat every range below as a planning buffer and **ask your supplier** for their current turnaround.

## Start with three dates, not one

Write these at the top of your notes:

1. **Wedding date**
2. **RSVP date** (usually a few weeks before final catering numbers are due)
3. **When out-of-town guests need to book travel and rooms**

Stationery timing hangs off those three. A Brisbane local guest can open an invitation six weeks out and be fine. A cousin flying from Perth or London needs earlier notice.

## A working-backwards timeline

Use this as a skeleton for a typical Australian celebration. Compress or stretch it once you have written quotes from your stationer or printer.

### 9–12 months before (or as soon as the date and city are firm)

**Save-the-dates**

- Useful when many guests must travel, or when your date sits on a long weekend or during peak summer holiday season.
- Share the city or region, the date, and a note that formal details will follow.
- Digital save-the-dates are common and fast; printed ones need design, print and postage time — ask your supplier how long they need after you approve the proof.

**Early decisions that unblock everything later**

- Names and spelling (including accents and hyphens)
- Ceremony and reception venues (even if times are still approximate)
- Whether you want a matched suite (invite, RSVP, menu, place cards) or mix-and-match pieces

### 5–7 months before

**Invitations — start the work even if you print later**

- Draft wording: hosts, couple's names, day, date, times, venues, dress note, RSVP method.
- Build the address list in a spreadsheet you can sort and mail-merge later.
- Choose paper, colour and printing method with humidity in mind if you're in Queensland or anywhere coastal and sticky (more on that below).
- Ask the supplier what their **proof → print → dispatch** sequence usually looks like in your month, and what happens if you change a spelling after approval.

### 3–4 months before

**Send invitations** (common planning window for many Australian weddings; overseas guests may need longer)

- Work backwards from your RSVP date, not from the wedding date alone.
- Add buffer for Australia Post delivery and for the weekend when you actually sit down to stamp envelopes.
- If you need calligraphy, wax seals or hand-assembly, those steps sit **after** printing — ask how long assembly takes for your guest count.

**Order or reserve day-of pieces in parallel**

- Menus
- Place cards or escort cards
- Table names or numbers
- Small signage that must match the suite (bar, gifts, guestbook)

Day-of stationery is easy to forget because it isn't posted. Starting it when invitations go to print avoids a last-minute mismatch.

### 4–6 weeks before

**Finalise menus and place cards**

- Lock spelling against your seating plan.
- Confirm dietary icons or short notes if you're including them.
- Ask about a small **reprint buffer** — a few spare place cards and two or three extra menus save panic when an RSVP changes late.

### After the wedding (plan the stationery before you leave for the honeymoon)

**Thank-you cards**

- Order cards (or confirm you have enough blanks) before the wedding if you want matching stationery.
- Address list and gift tracker should already exist; the bottleneck after the day is usually writing, not printing.

## Queensland summer: humidity, ink and outdoor weddings

If your wedding is in a humid climate — much of Queensland from late spring through summer — stationery choices affect how things look on the day, not just in the online proof.

**Paper and finishes**

- Very delicate papers and some letterpress impressions can soften or scuff in sticky air; ask your supplier what they recommend for humid venues.
- Dark envelopes in a hot car can curl; store finished stationery indoors in a dry spot, flat, until packing.
- Metallic foils and some inks can mark when guests' hands are warm and damp — test a sample with clean hands if you're unsure.

**Outdoor ceremonies**

- Place cards on uncovered tables can blow away or pick up condensation from drink stations. A heavier card stock, a clip, or a small escort-card display under cover helps.
- Menus left in direct sun may fade or warp; keep boxes shaded until setup.
- If you're attaching ribbon or wax seals, ask whether heat will soften the seal in transit to the venue.

**Proof on the paper you'll actually use**

Screen proofs hide how pale a colour looks in bright outdoor light. If your supplier can provide a printed proof or a sample set, it's worth it for outdoor summer weddings.

## What to proof — every time

Before you approve anything for print, check:

- Full names and preferred names (Bill vs William; hyphenated surnames)
- Date written out (day of week matching the calendar date)
- Venue names and street addresses
- Ceremony vs reception times and whether guests move between locations
- RSVP date and method (email, website, phone, card)
- Your contact person for guest questions
- Spelling on menu items and allergen notes
- Place-card names against the latest seating chart (one wrong table creates a queue at the door)

Have a second person read the proof cold. You will see what you expect to see; they will catch the missing "u" in honour / the Australian "personalised" debate / the venue suburb.

## Build a buffer for reprints and postage

Things that commonly eat a week you thought you had:

- A spelling change after the first proof
- A guest list jump when parents add cousins
- A reprint because the ink or paper wasn't what you imagined
- Public holidays around Easter, Christmas and mid-year depending on your state
- Interstate or rural postage taking longer than a local letter

**Practical buffers**

- Approve proofs earlier than the supplier's minimum, not on the last possible day.
- Order a small overage of invitations and day-of cards if the supplier allows it.
- Know Australia Post's current options for the weight of your finished envelope — a thick suite may need more than a regular stamp. Check their site rather than guessing.
- Keep digital printable backups of menus and place cards for true emergencies, even if your main suite is professionally printed.

Avoid relying on invented "standard" printer turnaround times from blogs (including this one). Two suppliers in the same city can differ by weeks in November and December. Get the date in writing in your quote.

## A simple order-of-operations checklist

1. Lock wedding date, city and rough guest geography.
2. Decide save-the-date: digital, printed, or neither.
3. Shortlist stationery style; request sample or detailed quote with timeline questions.
4. Build address spreadsheet and gift/RSVP tracker.
5. Draft invitation wording; collect venue exact names.
6. Proof → approve → print invitations; schedule assembly and posting.
7. Overlap day-of menus and place cards with the same visual suite.
8. Store everything flat and cool; pack a stationery emergency kit (spare place cards, tape, pens that match).
9. Pre-order or set aside thank-you cards before the honeymoon.

## What this guide deliberately skips

Favours, large outdoor signage boards, and the wording of thank-you notes are separate jobs with their own timing. So is calligraphy for envelope addressing. If you're stacking all of those on one supplier in peak spring wedding season, ask early whether they can take the full suite or only part of it.

Personalised stationery should reduce stress on the day, not create a second project that runs into the week before. Working backwards from the wedding date — with humidity, postage and reprint buffers built in — is the unglamorous habit that keeps the pretty paper on track.

*Ask your stationer or printer for their current lead times. Ranges in this guide are planning aids only, not promises.*
