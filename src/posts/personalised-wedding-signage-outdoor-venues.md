Signs do a surprising amount of work at a wedding. They welcome guests, point them to the ceremony, tell them where to sit, explain the unplugged ceremony, and label the bar, the dessert table and the photo booth. At an outdoor venue, they also have to cope with wind, sun, soft ground and the occasional storm.

This guide covers which signs are worth having, how to personalise them, and how to choose materials that survive an Australian outdoor wedding.

## Which signs do you actually need?

Not every wedding needs every sign. Start with the ones that solve real problems:

**Essential for most weddings**

- **Welcome sign:** your names and date, set at the entrance. Doubles as a photo spot.
- **Seating chart or table plan:** essential for receptions with assigned seating.
- **Directional signs:** vital for large or rural venues where the ceremony site isn't obvious from the car park.

**Useful depending on your day**

- **Unplugged ceremony sign:** asks guests to put phones away during the ceremony.
- **Order of events:** helps guests know when to expect the ceremony, speeches and cake.
- **Bar or drinks menu:** especially if you have signature cocktails.
- **"Choose a seat, not a side":** a friendly note for ceremony seating.
- **Guest book or wishing well sign.**
- **Reserved signs** for family seats.

**Nice extras**

- Neon or LED name signs for an evening reception.
- Dessert table, lolly bar or photo booth labels.
- A "Thank you for coming" sign at the exit.

## Materials for outdoor conditions

| Material | Pros | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Acrylic (clear or frosted) | Modern, lightweight, waterproof | Glare in direct sun; scratches; can be hard to read on busy backgrounds |
| Timber | Natural look, sturdy, photographs well | Heavy; can warp if left wet; needs sealing outdoors |
| Foam board | Cheap, light, easy to print | Blows over easily; damaged by rain; can curl in humidity |
| Corflute | Cheap, waterproof, light | Looks less premium; still blows over in wind |
| Mirror | Elegant, reflects the setting | Glare and reflections; heavy; fragile |
| Chalkboard | Editable, rustic | Rain washes it away; hard to read at distance |
| Fabric banner | Rolls up, easy to transport | Needs a frame or pole; flaps in wind |

### Glare is a real problem

Clear acrylic signs look stunning in photos but can be difficult to read in direct sun, especially with white text on a clear background. If your sign will face the afternoon sun:

- Choose frosted acrylic or add a solid backing board.
- Use bold, high-contrast lettering.
- Angle the sign away from the sun, or place it in shade.

### Wind is the bigger problem

Outdoor signs blow over. Many beach and hilltop venues are windy, especially in the afternoon. To reduce the risk:

- **Use heavy, wide-footed easels** rather than lightweight tripod easels.
- **Weigh the easel down** with sandbags, or clip it to something solid.
- **Choose rigid materials.** Foam board acts like a sail.
- **Keep signs lower to the ground** where wind is gentler, or lean them against a wall or tree with a support.
- **Ask your venue** how they usually secure signage; they'll know the local conditions.

### Rain and storms

Late-afternoon storms are common in south-east Queensland. Have a wet-weather plan for signage:

- Know where signs will go if the ceremony moves indoors.
- Choose waterproof materials for anything that will be outside for hours.
- Store paper-based signs in a plastic sleeve until the last minute.

## Personalising your signs

### Keep the wording clear

A sign is read from a distance and in passing. For a welcome sign, the essentials are:

> Welcome to the wedding of
> **Ash & Sam**
> 14 March 2027

Resist adding too much. Long quotes on a welcome sign often go unread.

### Make the seating chart easy to scan

The seating chart is the most-read sign of the day, by guests in a hurry and sometimes without their glasses.

- **List guests alphabetically by surname**, with their table, rather than table by table. People look for their own name first.
- **Use a large, simple font.** Script fonts are pretty but slow to read.
- **Split long lists into columns** with plenty of space.
- **Proof-read every name** against your final RSVP list. Ask someone else to check it too.
- **Have a backup plan** for last-minute changes: a few blank stickers and a pen in the set-up kit.

### Match your stationery

Use the same fonts and colours as your invitations so everything feels connected. One or two fonts are plenty.

### Personal touches guests love

- A sign introducing your dog (with a photo) if they're attending.
- A "Meet the wedding party" sign with names and how you know them.
- A short story of how you met, placed near the drinks where people linger.
- A map of the venue for larger properties.

## Practical set-up tips

- **Label everything.** Number signs and note where each goes so your coordinator or a friend can set up without you.
- **Pack a signage kit:** cable ties, clear tape, Blu Tack, scissors, sandbags or weights, and a cloth to wipe dust and fingerprints off acrylic.
- **Visit at the same time of day** as your wedding if you can, to see where the sun will be.
- **Check venue rules.** Some venues don't allow nails, tape on walls or stakes in the ground. Ask before you order anything that needs fixing.
- **Plan pack-down.** Assign someone to collect signs at the end of the night, especially if they're hired or sentimental.

## Timeline

- **4 to 6 months before:** decide which signs you need and the style. Get quotes and lead times.
- **2 to 3 months before:** finalise wording (except the seating chart).
- **4 to 6 weeks before:** finalise the seating chart once RSVPs are in.
- **2 weeks before:** collect or receive signs, check every word, and pack your set-up kit.

## After the wedding

A personalised welcome sign often finds a second life on a wall at home, especially timber or acrylic ones. If you think you'll want to keep it, choose a size that will fit somewhere in your house.

Good signage is the quiet backbone of a smooth day. Get the essentials right, plan for wind and weather, and keep the wording clear, and your guests will barely notice the signs at all, which is exactly the point.
