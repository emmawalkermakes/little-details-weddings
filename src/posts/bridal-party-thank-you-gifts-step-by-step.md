Your bridesmaids, groomsmen, flower kids and "person of honour" give up weekends, money and, quite often, their patience to help you get married. A thank-you gift won't repay that, and it doesn't have to. It just needs to say "I noticed what you did, and I know who you are."

That second part is where most of us get stuck. Matching robes and engraved hip flasks are easy to order in bulk, but they can feel like a uniform rather than a thank-you. Here's a step-by-step way to land on something personal without spending weeks deliberating.

## Step 1: Set the budget before you browse

Decide on a total, then divide it. Doing it the other way round, falling for a gift and working out the maths later, is how the thank-you budget quietly doubles.

It's fine to spend a different amount on different people. Your maid of honour who ran the hens' weekend may get a little more than a cousin who was a junior bridesmaid. Just keep the gifts *looking* roughly even if they'll be opened together.

## Step 2: Write one line about each person

Before you look at a single shop, write a short line for each person in your bridal party. Not what they like in general, but what they *did* and what they're like right now. For example:

- "Drove me to every dress fitting and never once complained about parking."
- "Just started running and won't stop talking about it."
- "Moving into their first place in January."
- "Has kept every concert ticket since high school."

These lines are your brief. A gift that connects to one of them will feel personal even if it's simple.

## Step 3: Pick a format: same, themed or individual

There are three workable approaches.

- **Same gift for everyone.** Easy and fair, and it works well when the gift is used on the day, like robes for getting ready or matching socks. Make it personal with names or initials, and a handwritten note for each.
- **Themed but different.** Everyone gets, say, "something for your home" or "an experience", but each one is chosen for the person. This keeps things looking even while letting you tailor them.
- **Fully individual.** The most personal, but also the most time-consuming. Best for a small bridal party.

If you have a mixed group (bridesmaids, groomsmen, a flower girl and a page boy), themed-but-different usually works best.

## Step 4: Choose from the brief, not from the shop

Go back to your one-liners and brainstorm two or three ideas for each person. Some starting points:

- **Things they'll use**: a good water bottle for the new runner, a nice kitchen knife or a set of glasses for someone setting up a first home.
- **Experiences**: a voucher for a class they've mentioned, tickets to a show, or a dinner out for two.
- **Keepsakes**: a framed photo of the two of you, a print of a place that matters to your friendship, or a scrapbook page with those concert tickets.
- **Personalised versions of everyday things**: a name or initial on something they'd buy anyway, such as a bag, a cup or a phone case.

For children in the bridal party, think about how old they'll be in a year. A book with a note inside the cover, or a small piece of jewellery to grow into, often lasts longer than a toy.

## Step 5: Order with a buffer

Anything personalised needs time. Check each seller's stated production and delivery times, then add a generous buffer, especially through November and December, when Australia Post and couriers are at their busiest and many small makers close over the holidays.

A few practical checks:

- **Spell-check every name** with the person's own spelling (ask casually if you're unsure).
- **Order one spare** of anything identical, in case one arrives damaged.
- **Keep the receipts and order numbers** in one place, so you can chase anything late without digging through emails.

## Step 6: Plan the handover and write the note

Decide *when* they'll get their gifts. Common choices:

- **The morning of the wedding**, while getting ready. This works for things they'll wear or use that day. It's also an emotional moment, so allow time and have tissues handy.
- **At the rehearsal or a dinner** in the days before, when everyone's a bit calmer.
- **After the wedding**, especially for experience gifts or framed photos from the day itself.

Whatever the gift, the note matters most. Use your one-liner from Step 2 as the starting point. "Thank you for driving me to every fitting and for not once mentioning the parking" will mean more than any engraving.

## A quick recap

1. Set the total budget, then divide it.
2. Write one line about each person.
3. Choose same, themed or individual.
4. Brainstorm from the brief, not the shop.
5. Order early, with a spare and a buffer.
6. Plan the handover and write a real note.

Small, thoughtful and on time beats big and generic every time.
