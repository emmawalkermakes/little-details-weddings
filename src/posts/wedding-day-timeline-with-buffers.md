Most wedding days don't run late because of one big disaster. They run late because of ten small things: hair took longer, the photographer needed five more minutes, a car got stuck behind a bus, someone couldn't find the rings. Each one is minor. Together they push the ceremony back half an hour and squeeze the light out of your photos.

A good timeline, sometimes called a run sheet, absorbs those small delays. Here's how to build one.

## Start with your fixed points

Some times on your wedding day can't move. Write those down first:

- **Ceremony start time**
- **Venue access times**: when you can get in to set up, and when you must be out
- **Sunset**, if you want outdoor photos in good light
- **Meal service**: the caterer will usually have a set window
- **Transport bookings**
- **Vendor finish times**: band, DJ, photographer, videographer

Everything else gets built around these.

## Work backwards from the ceremony

The ceremony is the anchor. Starting there, work backwards to the morning, asking at each step: "What needs to be finished before this can happen?"

For example, if the ceremony starts at 3.30pm, you might want to arrive at about 3.25pm. If the drive takes 25 minutes, you'll need to leave by 3.00pm. If you want a few relaxed photos before leaving, getting dressed needs to be done by 2.30pm, and so on, all the way back to the first hair and makeup appointment.

Then work forwards from the ceremony to the end of the night: congratulations, group photos, couple portraits, reception entry, meal, speeches, cake, first dance, last song, exit.

## Ask your vendors for real numbers

Don't guess durations. Ask each vendor directly:

- **Hair and makeup**: how long per person, and in what order? Ask who should go last (usually the person getting married, so everything stays fresh).
- **Photographer**: how much time do they want for family groups, wedding party photos and couple portraits? Do they want time with details like rings and invitations in the morning?
- **Celebrant**: how long will the ceremony run, including signing?
- **Caterer**: how long between courses? When do they need speeches to happen?
- **Venue**: when does the set-up crew need to finish, and when does the bar close?

Vendors who do weddings every weekend know their timings far better than any online template.

## Add buffers where days usually slip

This is the part most people skip. Build in a little spare time at the points that are most likely to run over:

- **Hair and makeup.** Add extra time to the end of the schedule, not the start. It's the single most common source of delay.
- **Getting dressed.** Buttons, zips, cufflinks, ties and corsets take longer than anyone thinks. Put the dress or suit on earlier than feels necessary.
- **Travel.** Check traffic for that day and time of the week, allow for parking, and remember that a convoy of cars moves slower than one.
- **Family photos.** Gathering people takes longer than taking the photos. A written list of groups and a family member who knows everyone helps hugely.
- **Between ceremony and reception.** Guests love to congratulate you, and you'll want to let them.

A buffer of a few minutes at each of these points is far easier to manage than a single big buffer at the end. If you end up ahead of time, nobody minds. You get a quiet ten minutes and a glass of water.

## Write it so people can follow it

A run sheet is only useful if the people running your day can read it quickly. Keep it simple:

| Time | What's happening | Where | Who's responsible |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3.00pm | Couple leaves for ceremony | Accommodation | Driver |
| 3.30pm | Ceremony begins | Garden | Celebrant |
| 4.00pm | Family group photos | Garden | Photographer + family helper |

Add columns for contact numbers if you're sharing it with vendors.

Then make two versions:

1. **The full run sheet** for your coordinator, MC, photographer and key vendors, with every detail and contact.
2. **A short version** for the wedding party and close family, with only the times and places they need to be.

Guests don't need the run sheet at all. They need the ceremony time and address on the invitation, and perhaps a rough timeline of the evening on your wedding website.

## Hand the timeline to someone else

On the day, you should not be the person checking the clock. Choose someone calm and organised to keep things moving: a coordinator, the venue manager, your MC or a trusted friend. Give them the run sheet a week or so before, walk them through it, and introduce them to the key vendors if you can.

Their job is to nudge things along, make small calls without bothering you, and decide when a delay doesn't matter.

## A final check before the day

About a week out, send the final run sheet to every vendor and ask them to reply with any changes. Then check:

- Do any two things need the same person at the same time?
- Is there time to eat? (For the couple, the wedding party and vendors.)
- Does the photographer have time for couple portraits in good light?
- Is there a moment, somewhere, where the two of you can just stand together and take it in?

That last one is worth putting in writing. Five minutes alone after the ceremony, before the crowd arrives, is one of the things couples often say they're glad they planned for.

A timeline won't stop things from happening on your wedding day. It just means that when they do, there's room for them.
