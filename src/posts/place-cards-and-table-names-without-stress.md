Place cards seem simple until you're doing them. Suddenly there are cousins whose names you're not sure how to spell, plus-ones who haven't confirmed, a late RSVP that changes three tables, and 120 little cards to sort by table on the night before your wedding.

This guide breaks the job into calm, manageable steps, with personalised ideas that double as favours and tips for getting it right on the day.

## Do you need place cards at all?

You have three options:

1. **Assigned seats (place cards at each setting).** Most control; most work.
2. **Assigned tables, free seating.** Guests find their table on the seating chart and sit anywhere at it. Less work, still organised.
3. **Free seating.** Easiest, but can be chaotic for larger weddings and awkward for guests who don't know many people.

If you're serving a plated meal with alternate drops or individual meal choices, assigned seats with place cards make life much easier for the catering team. For a cocktail or buffet reception, assigned tables or free seating are usually fine.

## Step 1: Get the names right

This is the most important step and the one that causes the most stress later.

- **Collect names in writing.** Ask for guests' names exactly as they'd like them on the RSVP. Online RSVPs make this easy.
- **Ask about preferred names.** "Robert" might go by "Bob", and "Katherine" might be "Kat".
- **Confirm plus-ones' names.** "Guest of Sarah" is less welcoming than the person's actual name.
- **Check tricky spellings** with a family member who knows.
- **Keep one master list** in a spreadsheet. Every sign, card and favour comes from this list, so it only needs fixing once.

## Step 2: Decide on table names

Numbered tables are clear and practical. But named tables are a lovely chance to personalise. Ideas:

- **Places that matter to you:** where you met, travelled or got engaged.
- **Brisbane or hometown spots:** suburbs, beaches, parks or favourite cafes.
- **Native flowers or plants:** Waratah, Banksia, Wattle, Grevillea.
- **Favourite songs, films or books.**
- **Your dogs, past and present.**

**Practical tip:** if you use names instead of numbers, add a number too ("Table 4: Byron Bay"). It helps the catering team and guests find their way.

## Step 3: Choose a format

### Classic folded cards

Cheap, easy to print at home, and fit any style. Use heavy card stock so they don't blow over. In humid weather, thin card can curl.

### Flat cards in holders

Stands or small holders keep cards upright in wind. Clips, mini easels, small stones and timber blocks all work.

### Place cards that double as favours

This is where personalisation shines, because it solves two jobs at once:

- **Personalised coasters** with each guest's name.
- **Name-printed stubby holders** set at each place.
- **Small acrylic or timber name tiles** guests can keep as a keyring or ornament.
- **Mini jars of honey or jam** with a name tag.
- **Seed packets** with the guest's name printed on the front.

If the place card *is* the favour, guests rarely leave it behind.

### Seating chart only

If you're doing assigned tables with free seating, you can skip individual cards altogether and invest in one excellent seating chart. See [personalised wedding signage for outdoor venues](/posts/personalised-wedding-signage-outdoor-venues/) for tips on making it readable.

## Step 4: Personalising without overwhelm

- **Choose one font pairing** that matches your invitations: one for names, one for table names.
- **Make names large enough** to read at arm's length. Guests often scan the table to find their seat.
- **Add meal codes discreetly** if needed. A small icon or coloured dot for dietary needs helps the catering team. Agree the system with your caterer.
- **Proof-read twice** against the master list. Ask someone else to check too.
- **Order a few spares** with blank space for last-minute guests, plus a good pen in the same colour.

## Step 5: Sorting for the day

The night-before panic usually comes from sorting, not making. Avoid it:

1. **Sort by table as soon as cards arrive**, not on the day.
2. **Bundle each table** in an envelope or zip-lock bag labelled with the table name and number.
3. **Include a list** of names in each bundle, so whoever sets up can check nothing is missing.
4. **Add a table map** showing where each table is in the room.
5. **Hand everything to your coordinator or a trusted friend.** Not you.

## Step 6: Outdoor and summer considerations

- **Wind:** use holders, weights or heavier items as place cards.
- **Humidity:** thin paper curls and ink can smudge. Use heavier card and test your printer ink.
- **Sun:** avoid setting tables with paper cards hours in advance in direct sun; they can fade or curl.
- **Rain:** if your reception is outdoors, have a plan for moving everything indoors quickly, including place cards.

## Handling last-minute changes

They will happen. A guest gets sick, a plus-one appears, two people need to be moved apart. Stay calm by preparing:

- Blank cards and a matching pen in the set-up kit.
- A printed final list with any late changes highlighted for the coordinator.
- One person (not you) who is the go-to for seating questions on the day.

## Timeline

- **3 months before:** choose format and table names; order holders or favour-style place cards.
- **6 weeks before:** RSVP deadline; finalise master list.
- **4 weeks before:** send final names to your maker, or print at home.
- **2 weeks before:** receive, check, sort and bundle by table.
- **On the day:** your coordinator or a friend sets up from the bundles.

Place cards aren't about perfection. They're about making each guest feel expected and welcome. Get the names right, keep it simple, and hand the set-up to someone else, and it'll be one of the easiest jobs on your list.
