Most wedding days run smoothly. The problems are almost always small: a button works loose, a hem catches on a heel, someone's contact lens goes missing, the cake knife is back at the hotel. None of these are disasters, but each one can pull a bride, groom or parent out of the moment for twenty minutes if nobody has the right thing to hand.

An emergency kit is a boring, practical answer. This post is a checklist you can adapt, plus a few thoughts on who should hold it and how to pack it so you can actually find things in a hurry.

## Decide who owns the kit

A kit only works if somebody is responsible for it. Pick one person, not a committee. A bridesmaid, a groomsman, a sibling or a calm friend who doesn't mind being a bit of a fixer works well. If you've hired a coordinator or stylist, ask what they already carry so you don't duplicate it; many professionals bring their own supplies.

Whoever it is, make sure they are:

- Staying close to you for most of the day, at least until the formalities are done.
- Not also doing a speech or a reading at a moment when you will need them.
- Clear on what is in the bag, so they can reach for it without asking.

Tell them the day before, not on the morning. A short message with the bag's contents is enough.

## Pack it in categories

Zip-lock bags or small pouches make a big difference. Label each one with a marker. A single jumble at the bottom of a tote bag is how you end up searching for safety pins while the photographer waits.

### 1. Clothing repairs

- Safety pins in a couple of sizes
- A small sewing kit with thread that matches your outfits (white, ivory, black, navy or whatever the wedding party wears)
- Fashion tape or double-sided tape for necklines and hems
- A lint roller, which is especially useful for dark suits and for anyone with a dog at home
- A small stain remover pen or wipe
- Spare buttons or cufflink backs if you have them
- Clear nail polish for stopping a small run in stockings

### 2. Comfort and body

- Blister plasters and a small roll of tape
- Insoles or heel grips, because new shoes are often the main enemy
- Pain relief tablets, for headaches and cramps, in their original packaging with your own dose instructions
- Antihistamines if you or anyone in the party has hay fever; spring and early summer are heavy pollen months in a lot of places
- Tissues or a small pack of handkerchiefs
- Any personal medication, taken as prescribed, kept somewhere secure but reachable (this one is for the person who needs it to pack themselves)
- Tampons or pads, in case anyone needs them
- A basic first aid kit: adhesive bandages, antiseptic wipes

### 3. Hair, make-up and grooming

- Bobby pins and hair elastics in colours that match the wedding party
- A small can of hairspray (check venue rules around sprays if you're in a small room)
- A travel comb or brush
- Your lipstick and powder for touch-ups
- Oil-blotting sheets if you tend to shine
- A small mirror
- Deodorant
- Mints or gum, and a toothbrush and toothpaste if you'll be out until late

### 4. Weather items (adjust for your venue)

Australian weddings throw all sorts of conditions at you. A beach ceremony in November and a garden reception in a humid week are not the same job.

- **Sun:** sunscreen, sunglasses and hats for guests waiting outdoors. If the ceremony is in the afternoon, a few cheap fans can be a kindness.
- **Heat:** water, preferably in a cooler, and a small hand towel. A few spare handkerchiefs for sweat are more useful than people expect.
- **Humidity:** anti-frizz serum, a travel-size deodorant, and blotting papers.
- **Wind:** extra bobby pins, spare hair ties for the wedding party, and clips or weights to hold down order-of-service cards or signage.
- **Rain:** a few large umbrellas, in a neutral colour, plus towels to wipe off chairs. In Queensland and northern Australia, afternoon storms in the warmer months are common enough that a wet-weather plan is worth discussing with your venue.
- **Insects:** insect repellent, particularly for outdoor receptions near water or gardens around dusk.

### 5. Ceremony and reception bits

- Pens, including one that is reliable on the marriage documents (check with your celebrant what they will provide)
- The rings, and a plan for where they live before the ceremony (they're best kept in a secure pocket, not in the kit)
- Spare copies of the order of the day, vows, speeches and readings, in case someone's phone dies
- A small roll of sticky tape and a pair of scissors
- A cake knife and a server, if the venue isn't providing them
- Lighter or matches, if you are using candles and your venue allows them
- Ribbon, cable ties and a small roll of masking tape, for fixing signage or flowers
- Spare place cards, blank, in case of a last-minute change
- Cash in small notes, in an envelope, for unexpected gratuities or small purchases
- A phone charger and power bank

### 6. Food and drink for you

Wedding days can run long between mealtimes. The bride and groom frequently say afterwards that they barely ate. Pack:

- Muesli bars, nuts or similar snacks that don't melt
- Water bottles
- A couple of straws, if the bride is wearing lipstick and wants to drink without messing it up

## What not to pack

- **Anything that melts in a hot car.** Chocolate, certain lipsticks and some adhesives can fail in a hot vehicle. Keep the kit in the air-conditioned car or the bridal room, not in the boot.
- **Duplicates of what the venue supplies.** Ask your coordinator or venue what is already on-site.
- **Anything you wouldn't want to lose.** The rings, legal documents and valuables should be carried by trusted people, not left in a shared bag.

## Make the kit small enough to carry

Resist the temptation to pack a second wardrobe. A medium tote or a small box is plenty. The point is to cover the most common small problems, not every possible one. If you have a lot of items, split them into two: a "bride and groom" bag that stays close, and a "venue" bag with the tape, scissors and spares that stays in the car or the reception room.

## A short timeline for putting it together

- **Two weeks before:** Write the list. Buy anything you don't have.
- **One week before:** Pack the pouches and label them. Test the sewing kit: thread a needle now, not on the day.
- **The day before:** Brief the person who is looking after it. Check the weather forecast and add or remove weather items.
- **The morning of:** Put the kit in the car or the bridal room and tell the person where it is.
- **After the reception:** Hand the kit back to whoever owns it, and take any leftover items to be returned or thrown out.

If you're still sorting out the personalised details themselves, such as signs, favours and place cards, the [personalisation checklist](/checklist/) is a good companion to this one.

## One last thing

The most useful item in the kit is not on any list. It is a person whose job is to say "I've got this". If something goes wrong, the kit and the person together let you carry on and enjoy the day. Pack the kit once, hand it over, and then forget about it. It will probably come home unopened, and that is the best outcome.
