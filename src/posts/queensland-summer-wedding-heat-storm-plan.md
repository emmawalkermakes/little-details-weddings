Summer weddings in Queensland can be beautiful: long light, warm evenings, everyone in linen. They can also be hot, sticky and interrupted by a storm that rolls in at exactly the wrong moment.

The difference between those two days usually comes down to planning for the weather you're likely to get, not the weather in the photos you've saved. This guide works through it in order: timing, the ceremony, guest comfort, storms, and the small details that wilt.

## 1. Time the day around the heat

The hottest part of a summer day is usually the early to mid-afternoon. A ceremony at 2pm in an open garden can be hard going for guests in suits, older relatives and anyone standing in the sun.

Some ways to work around it:

- **Push the ceremony later.** A late-afternoon ceremony often catches cooler air and softer light. Queensland doesn't use daylight saving, so summer sunsets come earlier than they do in Sydney or Melbourne. Look up the sunset time for your exact date and location (Geoscience Australia and several weather sites publish them) and plan backwards.
- **Or go early.** A morning ceremony followed by a long lunch is an underrated summer option and avoids the typical afternoon storm window.
- **Keep the outdoor part short.** If the ceremony is outside, plan to move guests into shade or air-conditioning for the reception.
- **Talk to your photographer about light.** They'll know when the light is best at your venue and can suggest how much time you'll need for portraits before sunset.

## 2. Set up the ceremony for comfort

Walk your ceremony site at the same time of day as your ceremony, ideally around the same time of year. Look at where the sun will be, which way guests will face, and where the shade falls.

- **Face guests away from the sun** if you can, so they're not squinting through the vows.
- **Ask about shade.** Trees, a pergola, a marquee or market umbrellas can all help. Shade over the couple and celebrant matters too.
- **Set up a water station** near where guests arrive, with cups or bottles, and keep it topped up.
- **Offer fans and sunscreen.** A basket of paper fans and a pump bottle of sunscreen near the entrance gets used. If you'd like to personalise the fans with your names or the date, keep it simple and order them early.
- **Reserve shaded seats** for older guests, pregnant guests, anyone with a health condition, and young children.
- **Keep it moving.** A ceremony doesn't need to be long to be meaningful. Talk to your celebrant about a comfortable length for an outdoor summer setting.

## 3. Tell guests what to expect

Guests can only prepare for the conditions if they know about them. In your invitation, wedding website or a note closer to the date, mention:

- whether the ceremony is outdoors and on grass, sand or uneven ground (useful for shoe choices)
- that it's likely to be warm, and that lighter fabrics are welcome
- whether there's shade, and whether hats are fine during the ceremony
- where the reception will be and whether it's air-conditioned

If you have a dress code, consider how it works in summer heat. "Cocktail" in January in Queensland can look very different to "cocktail" in July.

## 4. Build a real wet-weather plan

Summer in south-east Queensland is storm season. Afternoon and evening thunderstorms can bring heavy rain, strong winds, hail and lightning, sometimes with little warning. Further north, summer is also the wet season, and the official tropical cyclone season runs from November to April.

A wet-weather plan shouldn't be an afterthought. Before you sign with a venue, ask:

- **What's the indoor or covered alternative,** and how many guests does it hold comfortably?
- **Who makes the call to move inside, and by when?** Many venues set a decision time on the morning of the wedding.
- **Is there a cost** for setting up the backup space or for a marquee?
- **If there's a marquee, how does it cope with wind and heavy rain?** Ask the hire company about its wind limits and what happens if those are exceeded.
- **How will guests move between spaces** if it's raining? Covered paths, umbrellas, golf buggies?

Then make your own plan for the day:

- **Keep an eye on the forecast** in the week before, and check the Bureau of Meteorology for warnings on the day.
- **Have umbrellas ready.** Clear or white umbrellas photograph well and are easy to borrow or hire.
- **Brief your coordinator or MC** so they can make the call and move guests calmly if a storm arrives.
- **Take lightning seriously.** If you hear thunder, it's time to get everyone inside or under proper cover, not under a tree or a small gazebo.
- **Check your wedding insurance,** if you have it, and read what it says about weather and cancellations.

## 5. Protect the things that wilt

Heat and humidity are hard on more than guests.

- **Flowers:** ask your florist which blooms hold up in heat, and keep bouquets and buttonholes cool and out of the sun until they're needed.
- **The cake:** buttercream and some icings soften in heat. Ask your baker what suits a summer wedding and where the cake will be kept until it's cut.
- **Paper goods:** humidity can make paper signs and seating charts curl. Mount them on a rigid backing and keep them under cover.
- **Candles:** tall candles can bend in heat, and open flames don't mix well with outdoor breezes. Lanterns or hurricane glass help.
- **Hair and makeup:** talk to your artists about products that last in humidity, and keep a small touch-up kit handy.

## 6. Little extras that make a big difference

- **Insect repellent** in the bathrooms and near outdoor areas, especially around dusk and near water.
- **Cold towels** for the wedding party after photos.
- **A cooling spot** for anyone who feels unwell: somewhere shaded or air-conditioned, with water and a chair.
- **Plenty of water at the reception,** on every table and at the bar.
- **Shoes for the dance floor** if your day shoes aren't made for a long, warm night.

## A quick summer-wedding checklist

- [ ] Ceremony timed to avoid the hottest part of the day
- [ ] Sunset time checked for the date and location
- [ ] Site visit at ceremony time to check sun and shade
- [ ] Water station, fans and sunscreen organised
- [ ] Guests told about outdoor conditions and dress
- [ ] Wet-weather plan confirmed in writing with the venue
- [ ] Decision time and decision-maker agreed
- [ ] Florist and baker briefed on summer conditions
- [ ] Signs mounted and kept under cover
- [ ] Insect repellent and a cool-down space ready

A summer wedding doesn't need perfect weather. It needs a plan that means you can stop checking the radar and enjoy the day, whatever the sky does.
