Wedding favours have a reputation problem. Too many end up left on tables at the end of the night, or worse, melted. A sugared almond in a tulle bag doesn't stand a chance at a 33-degree garden reception in Brisbane, and a beautifully labelled candle can slump in a hot car before it reaches the venue.

The good news is that personalised favours can be both useful and heat-proof. This guide covers what works in a Queensland (or any Australian) summer, what to avoid, and how to handle favours on the day.

## What makes a favour "summer-proof"?

Before choosing, run every idea past four questions:

1. **Will it melt, soften or sweat?** Chocolate, wax, soft lollies and some soaps all suffer above the mid-20s, especially in a car or a marquee.
2. **Will humidity ruin it?** Paper tags can curl, unsealed timber can swell, and some printed finishes go tacky.
3. **Will guests actually take it home?** Useful, small and easy to carry beats elaborate and fragile.
4. **Can it sit out for hours?** Favours are often set out well before guests arrive.

## Personalised favours that cope with heat

### Stubby holders with your names and date

They're practically the unofficial favour of Australian summer weddings for a reason: they're cheap, light, genuinely useful on the day, and people take them home. Keep the design simple: your first names, the date, maybe a small motif. Choose neoprene with a stitched base for durability.

**Tip:** if a lot of guests don't drink, they work just as well on soft drink cans and water bottles.

### Personalised hand fans

At an outdoor ceremony in summer, a fan is a favour that does its job in the first five minutes. Paper or timber fans can be printed with your names and date, or with the order of service on the back. Place them on ceremony chairs instead of at the reception.

### Sunscreen or SPF lip balm with custom labels

Practical, used immediately at an outdoor ceremony, and suitable for everyone. Buy from an established brand and add your own label to the outside, rather than decanting into unlabelled containers. Check use-by dates and keep them out of direct sun.

### Personalised coasters

A coaster with your initials or a simple design is useful long after the wedding, and doubles as a place card if you print guests' names on them (more on that in [place cards and table names without the stress](/posts/place-cards-and-table-names-without-stress/)). Cork, acrylic and timber all handle heat well.

### Seed packets or seedlings

Native seeds or easy herbs like basil with a personalised label are a lovely, low-waste favour. If you're inviting interstate or overseas guests, be aware of quarantine and biosecurity rules on carrying seeds and plants between states and into other countries; packaged commercial seeds are safer than loose seed, and live seedlings are best kept for local guests.

### Mini bottles of local hot sauce, honey or jam

Glass jars and bottles cope with heat better than chocolate. Choose shelf-stable products, check for allergen information on the label, and add your own tag. Honey can crystallise or get runny but won't spoil.

### Personalised keyrings or bottle openers

Small, durable and easy to carry. A bottle opener with your names and date ties in nicely if you're also giving stubby holders.

### Sunglasses with a printed arm

For a beach or garden wedding, cheap sunglasses with your names printed on the arm are fun for photos and useful on the day. Keep expectations modest on quality and offer them in a basket rather than one per seat.

## Favours to think twice about in summer

| Favour | The summer problem | Workaround |
|---|---|---|
| Chocolates | Melt quickly, even indoors near windows | Keep chilled until the last minute, or choose a different treat |
| Candles | Soften in cars and marquees; can bend | Store indoors in air-con; keep out of the sun |
| Bath bombs and soaps | Can absorb humidity and sweat | Wrap in sealed bags; set out late |
| Macarons and iced biscuits | Icing weeps in humidity | Serve as dessert instead of take-home |
| Fresh flowers | Wilt within hours in heat | Give potted succulents or dried flowers |

## On-the-day logistics

**Keep favours cool until set-up.** Store them indoors and away from windows. A car parked in the sun can get much hotter than the outside air, so don't leave favours in a car between venues.

**Set them out late.** Ask your coordinator to put favours out as close to guest arrival as possible, especially for heat-sensitive items.

**Use shade.** At outdoor receptions, place the favour table under cover rather than in full sun.

**Have a plan for leftovers.** Some favours will always be left behind. Nominate someone to collect them at the end of the night.

**Factor in storms.** Late-afternoon storms are common in south-east Queensland in spring and summer. Paper items and fabric bags left on outdoor tables won't survive a downpour.

## Personalisation that looks good

- **Keep text short.** First names and the date are plenty. Long quotes rarely fit nicely on small items.
- **Pick one font pairing** and use it across favours, signage and stationery for a cohesive look.
- **Choose a date format and stick to it.** "14.03.2027", "14 March 2027" and "March 14" all look different. Australian style is day-month-year.
- **Proof-read twice**, including the spelling of both your names.

## Timeline for ordering favours

- **3 to 4 months before:** decide on favour type, test one in summer conditions (leave a sample in a warm room for a day).
- **2 to 3 months before:** confirm guest numbers and order. Add a few spares.
- **1 month before:** check the delivered order, count, and store somewhere cool.
- **Week of the wedding:** pack favours with set-up instructions for whoever's placing them.

## The best favour is the one that gets used

A favour doesn't need to be expensive. It needs to suit your day, survive the weather and leave guests with something they'll actually use. In a Queensland summer, that usually means something to keep drinks cold, faces shaded or tables protected.
