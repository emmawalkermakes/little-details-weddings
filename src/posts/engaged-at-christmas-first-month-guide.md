The weeks between Christmas and New Year are a popular time to get engaged. Families are together, work is quiet, and there's a natural sense of a fresh start. If that's you this summer, congratulations.

It's also a time when everyone around you suddenly has questions. *When's the wedding? Where? Am I invited?* By Boxing Day, you might have three venue suggestions from an aunt and a cousin offering to be the photographer.

None of that has to be answered in the first month. Here's a gentle guide to what's worth doing early, and what can happily wait until the champagne's worn off.

## Week one: enjoy it, and tell people in the order you'd like

The first week isn't for planning. It's for telling the people who matter, in the way you'd like them to hear it.

**Decide on the order together.** If parents, grandparents or close friends would be hurt to find out from social media, tell them first, ideally by phone, video call or in person. Christmas gatherings make this easy for some families and awkward for others. If you'd rather not announce it in front of twenty relatives at lunch, that's completely fine.

**Hold off on posting until the inner circle knows.** It saves a lot of "I had to find out on Instagram" conversations.

**Have a stock answer ready** for the inevitable "So when's the wedding?":

> "We're just enjoying being engaged for now. We'll start planning in the new year."

Say it warmly and often. It's true, and it closes the subject without offending anyone.

A new ring and an Australian summer holiday aren't always a great mix. Beaches, pools, sunscreen and hot days all bring their own risks.

- **Check the fit.** Fingers can swell in heat and shrink in cold water, so a ring that feels fine indoors may slip off in the surf. If it feels loose, ask the jeweller about resizing. Many close over the Christmas period, so check their holiday hours.
- **Take it off for swimming, the beach and sport.** Cold water, sunscreen and sand are a risky combination. Have one safe spot for it (a small dish at home, or a zipped pocket in your bag) and always use the same one.
- **Sort out insurance.** Find out whether your contents insurance covers jewellery away from home, or whether you need to list it separately. Keep the receipt or valuation somewhere safe.
- **Keep the box**, at least for now. It's handy for travel.

## Weeks two and three: the big-picture conversation

Once things settle down, set aside an evening for the two of you. No spreadsheets yet, just a conversation. Questions worth talking through:

1. **What kind of wedding do we picture?** Big or small? City, country, beach or backyard? Formal or relaxed?
2. **Roughly when?** A season or a year is enough for now. Remember that in Queensland, summer brings heat, humidity and storms, while the cooler months are popular and book out early.
3. **Who's paying for what?** If family might contribute, have that conversation before anyone books anything.
4. **What matters most to each of us?** Pick your top two or three priorities each, such as food, photos, music or having everyone there. These will guide every decision later on.
5. **What don't we care about?** This list is just as useful. Anything on it is a place to save money and stress.

Write the answers down. In six months, when you're comparing two very different venues, you'll be glad you did.

## Week four: light, low-commitment tasks

If you're itching to start, here are a few things that are useful but don't lock you into anything.

- **Draft a rough guest list.** Just names in a shared note. Guest numbers drive almost every cost, so knowing whether you're closer to 40 or 140 helps enormously.
- **Start a single place for ideas.** A shared folder, a notebook or a board. One place, not five.
- **Note the dates that matter.** Family birthdays, school holidays if guests have kids, major events in your area, and anyone's planned overseas travel.
- **Visit a wedding expo or open day** if one's coming up and you enjoy that sort of thing. Go to look and collect ideas, not to sign up for anything on the spot.

## What can wait

It's tempting to lock things in while you're excited, but a few decisions are better made after the big-picture conversation.

- **Booking a venue.** It's the decision that sets your date, guest numbers and much of your budget. Take your time.
- **Choosing your bridal party.** Asking someone on Boxing Day and then rethinking it in February is awkward for everyone.
- **Save-the-dates.** You need a confirmed date and venue first.
- **Big deposits** of any kind. Read the cancellation terms before you pay.

## One piece of admin to keep in mind

In Australia, you'll need to give your authorised celebrant a Notice of Intended Marriage at least one month and no more than 18 months before the wedding. You don't need to do anything about it yet. Just know that it exists, so a short engagement doesn't catch you out. The [Attorney-General's Department](https://www.ag.gov.au/families-and-marriage/marriage/get-married) has the current details.

## An engagement party, or not?

Some families expect one; others don't. There's no rule. If you'd like one, a relaxed get-together in the new year (once everyone's back from holidays) is often easier than squeezing it into the busy Christmas fortnight. If you'd rather not, a few dinners with the important people work just as well.

## The short version

- **Week one:** tell your people in your own order, and practise "We'll plan in the new year."
- **Ring:** check the fit, keep it away from the surf, and sort the insurance.
- **Weeks two and three:** have the big-picture talk and write it down.
- **Week four:** draft a guest list and gather ideas, but don't commit.
- **Wait on:** venue, bridal party, save-the-dates and deposits.

You'll spend months planning a wedding. You only get a few weeks of simply being newly engaged, so enjoy them.
